# One bridge

## Context

The page has to answer one question: did your choices beat buying one fund on your own dates, and which choices made the difference? An earlier write-up answered that with three separate results, and with a fund path that paid no distributions. That write-up is withdrawn. A reader of three separate results cannot check that they describe the same history.

## Decision

There is one bridge. Four paths are valued on the as-of date: all on day one, fund on your dates, the benchmark, and your account. The three steps are the gaps between neighbours.

1. When the money arrived is fund on your dates minus all on day one.
2. Cash and sales is the benchmark minus fund on your dates.
3. Stock selection is your account minus the benchmark, and it equals the sum of the pick impacts.

The headline is cash and sales plus stock selection, which is your account minus fund on your dates. All on day one plus the three steps equals your account. In the walkthrough that is $9,000, then a loss of $600, a loss of $600, and a gain of $1,200, which lands on $9,000. The headline is a gain of $600.

## Alternatives

Three separate answers lost. Nothing forced them to add up, so a mistake in one could not be caught by the other two.

One headline and no steps lost. The headline says whether you beat the fund on your dates. It does not say whether that came from the day the money arrived, from cash left uninvested and from sales, or from the stocks you picked. The page is those three steps.

A percentage return lost for this version. The steps are dollar gaps, and dollar gaps add exactly. A rate would need a choice of which dollars count as the base, and two different bases would not add back to the headline.

## Cost

The replay runs four times. All on day one is a counterfactual: it treats later contributions as if that cash had already existed on the first contribution date, and the first step is the cost of that fiction. The headline is a dollar gain, so the same $600 on a small history and on a large one is the same headline. Each lot is rounded once, and the account total may sit up to half a cent per lot from one exact rounding. The bridge still adds exactly, because every step is a difference of sums of those cents.
